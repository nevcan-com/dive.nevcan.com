## Karstdive ’95

In the summer of 1995 the Woodville Karst Plain Project (WKPP) and Türkiye’s Underwater Research
Society (SAD) ran Karstdive, a joint research and exploration project with Hacettepe University’s
karst water research centre (UKAM), the University of Wyoming, the State Hydraulic Works (DSİ) in
Antalya and Kepez Electric. Its aim was to explore, map and document underwater cave systems
along the southern flank of the Taurus Mountains, and to help understand the region’s
groundwater.

The team was small: Todd Kincaid and Jarrod Jablonski from Florida, with Gökhan Türe and Zafer
Kızılkaya of SAD. In eight days underwater they explored five cave systems and reached 117 m in
Gök Cave, near Finike. Kincaid, Jablonski and Barry Miller returned in 1996. GUE counts Karstdive
among the work that laid its own foundation.

## KarstDive: Western Taurus

Three decades on, KarstDive Western Taurus picks up that work in the same mountains: exploring,
surveying and documenting their underwater caves. I am the project’s manager. It is in planning,
and its first season will be reported here.
